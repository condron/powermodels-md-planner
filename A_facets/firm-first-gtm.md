# Firm-First Go-To-Market Model

## Description

PowerModels uses a deliberate, leveraged go-to-market strategy: sell to accounting firms, not individual businesses. A single firm adoption decision unlocks expansion across dozens or hundreds of client entities under the firm's control. This creates a fundamentally different scaling model than traditional SMB software sales.

## Key Points

- **Buyer = Accounting firm**, not individual business
- **Single sale → many deployments**: One firm decision expands to their entire client portfolio
- **Firm-controlled rollout**: Firms standardize preparation once, then apply across clients
- **Internal expansion > external selling**: Growth driven by firm adoption depth, not new customer acquisition
- **Trust-based adoption**: Firms pilot with 3-5 high-pain clients, then expand based on confidence
- **No per-client negotiation**: Pricing and deployment governed by firm license, not individual contracts

## GTM Mechanics

1. **Sell to firm leadership**: Target firm partners/principals with economic decision authority
2. **Pilot with 3-5 clients**: High-pain, trusted client relationships for initial validation
3. **Prove value in one cycle**: Demonstrate preparation efficiency and trust in tax season
4. **Firm standardizes**: Tool becomes default for additional clients (firm-controlled)
5. **Expand organically**: Revenue grows as firm onboards more clients to standard workflow

## Unit Economics Leverage

- **CAC**: Incurred once per firm (not per client)
- **Revenue**: Expands as firm adds clients to platform
- **Retention**: Driven by workflow integration and record reuse, not contractual lock-in
- **Growth efficiency**: Linear sales effort → exponential client coverage through firm portfolios

## Differentiation from SMB SaaS

| Traditional SMB SaaS | PowerModels Firm-First |
|---------------------|------------------------|
| Sell to each business | Sell to firm once |
| Linear CAC per customer | Amortized CAC across portfolio |
| Self-serve adoption | Professional-led deployment |
| High churn risk | Workflow lock-in via firm |
| Marketing-driven growth | Trust and pilot-driven growth |

## Strategic Implications

- **Speed measured differently**: Time to first completed cycle + firm rollout depth, not logo count
- **Capital efficiency**: Growth funded by firm expansion, not per-SMB acquisition spending
- **Quality over velocity**: Deeper adoption in fewer firms > shallow adoption in many businesses
- **Durability**: Once standardized in firm workflow, expansion becomes mechanical not promotional

## Risks

- **Longer initial sales cycle**: Must prove value to firm, not quick self-serve signup
- **Firm dependency**: Loss of one firm = loss of many clients
- **Requires trust**: Cannot force adoption; must earn confidence through pilot results
