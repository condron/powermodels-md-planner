# Unit Economics & Capital Efficiency

## Description

PowerModels' firm-first GTM model creates fundamentally different unit economics than traditional SMB SaaS. Instead of linear CAC per customer, acquisition cost is amortized across entire firm client portfolios, creating a highly leveraged growth model.

## Core Economic Model

### Revenue Structure
- **Pricing**: $50-150/entity/month (firm-licensed, per-client subscription)
- **Firm-controlled**: Firm pays for clients they onboard
- **Conservative pricing**: Aligned to workload, not seats or files
- **Monthly billing**: Predictable recurring revenue

### Cost Structure
- **CAC**: Incurred once per firm (not per client)
- **Revenue expansion**: As firm adds clients to platform
- **Retention driver**: Workflow integration and record reuse (not contractual lock-in)
- **Sales efficiency**: One firm sale → many client deployments

## The Leverage Mechanism

```
Traditional SMB SaaS:          PowerModels Firm-First:
CAC × N customers              CAC × 1 firm
Revenue × N                    Revenue × (clients per firm)
Linear scaling                 Leveraged scaling
```

### Example Economics
- **Firm CAC**: $25K (pilot + sales cycle)
- **Initial pilot**: 5 clients @ $100/month = $500/month
- **Year 1 expansion**: 20 clients @ $100/month = $2,000/month
- **Year 2 expansion**: 50 clients @ $100/month = $5,000/month
- **CAC payback**: < 12 months, then pure expansion

Compare to SMB SaaS:
- 50 individual sales @ $2K CAC each = $100K total CAC
- Same $5K/month revenue
- 20-month payback vs 12-month

## Path to Breakeven

**Base operating plan reaches cash-flow breakeven through:**
1. Firm adoption (not new product lines)
2. Client rollout within firms (not pricing expansion)
3. Standardization and reuse (not high-velocity selling)

**No reliance on:**
- New modules or upsells
- Price increases
- Venture-scale marketing spend
- SMB self-serve funnels

## Capital Efficiency Drivers

### Low Customer Acquisition Cost (per revenue dollar)
- Sell once to firm → expand across portfolio
- Trust-based adoption → lower sales friction
- Professional networks → referral-driven growth

### High Customer Lifetime Value
- Workflow integration → sticky retention
- Multi-year standardization → durable relationships
- Expansion within firm → growing ARPU

### Predictable Growth
- Growth constrained by onboarding capacity, not marketing spend
- Disciplined scaling (not growth-at-all-costs)
- Organic expansion (firm-controlled, not sales-pushed)

## Key Metrics

### Firm-Level
- **Firms acquired** (logo count)
- **Clients per firm** (expansion depth)
- **Time to first client onboard**
- **Time to 10+ clients per firm**
- **Firm retention rate**

### Economic
- **CAC per firm**
- **Average clients per firm** (expansion metric)
- **Revenue per firm** (MRR and growth rate)
- **CAC payback period**
- **LTV : CAC ratio**

### Efficiency
- **Burn multiple** (dollars burned per net new ARR)
- **Magic number** (ARR added per sales/marketing dollar)
- **Months of runway**

## Strategic Implications

### Growth Velocity
- **Speed measured by**: Time to completed cycle + rollout depth (not logo count)
- **Expansion is mechanical**: Once firm standardizes, growth becomes operational not promotional
- **Quality over quantity**: 10 deep firm relationships > 100 shallow SMB customers

### Capital Requirements
- **Lower burn**: No high-velocity SMB acquisition engine needed
- **Longer runway**: Disciplined growth extends cash runway
- **Milestone-driven**: Capital accelerates proven motion, doesn't fund speculation

### Investor Appeal
- **Capital efficient**: Leveraged model reduces dilution
- **Durable growth**: Firm relationships are sticky and compound
- **Clear path to profitability**: Not dependent on constant fundraising

## Risks

- **Slower initial growth**: Longer sales cycles, deeper relationships
- **Firm concentration**: Loss of one firm = loss of many clients
- **Onboarding capacity**: Growth constrained by ability to deliver quality implementations
