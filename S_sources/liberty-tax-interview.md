Firm & Client Profile
Liberty Tax franchise with an all-year business, not just seasonal
Handles both Canadian and US taxes
Clients are generally transparent and willing to provide bank access
Approximately 40% of clients are small businesses (e.g., Uber drivers)
Roughly every third client is a business client
Client mix includes:
Construction and restoration companies (5–6)
IT companies (3)

Real estate agent (1)

Event company (1)
Photography company (1)
Currently:
4–5 corporate clients
Growing by approximately 6 companies per year
About 20–25 clients require ongoing bookkeeping help

Current Workflow (Bookkeeping & Tax)
• QuickBooks Online is standard; clients pay their own subscriptions
• Typical onboarding for a new business includes:
12 months of bank statements
Approximately 3 bank accounts
Income and expenses only (simple cases)
Target turnaround of about 7 days
• Workflow is:
Bookkeeper enters data
Arishma reviews financials
Typically 2–3 companies onboarded at a time
• Heavy use of judgment and “common sense” depending on business type
• Excel outputs are often provided to clients

Pricing Model (Context)
• Bookkeeping is packaged, not billed hourly
• For simple new businesses:
Bookkeeping typically priced at $2,000–$2,500
Combined bookkeeping + tax package around $4,500
Standalone bookkeeping sometimes quoted as low as $1,200
• Taxes without bookkeeping:
$800–$1,000
• Small business filings (e.g., Uber drivers):
$160–$300, completed quickly and inclusive of GST
• Payroll:
Uses QuickBooks Payroll
Payroll is done manually
Approximately $120 per month
Payroll automation seen as a “nice-to-have” rather than core

Operational & Financial Notes
• Significant royalty fees paid to Liberty:
Approximately $180k in personal tax revenue
Approximately $40k paid in royalties
• Liberty provides mass marketing only
No operational guidance
No support on how to run the accounting business
• Off-season profit is approximately $25k per month
• Overall profile: profitable, capacity-constrained, and process-heavy

Pain Points & Complexity Signals (Relevant to Us)
• Heavy reliance on manual review, QBO rules, and judgment calls
• Common complexities include:
Multiple businesses per owner
Transfers between Liberty accounts and operating cash
Shareholder funds being injected into businesses
Requirement to track each shareholder in a separate account
• Bank access is not a blocker
• Willing to:
Share one month of real data for extraction
Review what emerges from the system
Participate in an intake interview to define rules

Why This Is Valuable to Us

• Comfortable sharing real bank data
