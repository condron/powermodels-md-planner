## 2) Investor-grade evaluation of PowerModels vs market

### A) What’s very strong (keep, amplify)

**1) The wedge is coherent and defensible**
“Tax-driven preparation is the forcing function” is a *real* buyer urgency lever. It’s a deadline-driven, correctness-forcing workflow that firms already budget for.

**2) The 85% boundary is unusually smart**
This is one of your biggest investor advantages because it:

* prevents “replace the accountant” backlash,
* avoids liability landmines,
* and creates a clean scope boundary that protects margins.

This matches the broader industry direction: AI becomes a native layer inside existing accounting workflows, not a replacement for professional judgment. ([Accounting Today][2])

**3) The GTM motion is structurally attractive**
“Sell once per firm, expand inside the firm” is exactly the kind of compounding unit economics seed investors like—*if* you can prove onboarding scales and expansion is real.

### B) Where investors will push hard (fix these in the plan/deck)

**1) “Proven completeness” needs a crisp definition**
Right now it’s compelling but a bit abstract. Investors will ask:

* *Completeness against what?* Bank/CC statements only? Cash-basis? Accrual adjustments?
* *What is the acceptance test?* (“Statement line items reconciled to journal transactions” is good—make it measurable.)
* *What’s the failure mode?* (missing statements, duplicated feeds, ambiguous merchant descriptors, etc.)

**Make this a hard spec**: a one-page “Completeness Contract” with pass/fail criteria.

**2) Roadmap gates are good—but dates are already stale**
Your Gate 1 is listed as **Jan 15, 2026** (already past as of Feb 14, 2026). That creates immediate investor doubt.

Fix: keep the *gate concept*, but present as **current status**:

* Gate 1: **Achieved / In progress / Slipped** with proof artifact (demo recording, runbook, test dataset).
* Gate 2: what’s left, and what evidence you’ll show.

**3) Pricing is plausible but currently unproven**
$100/client/month can work, but firms will benchmark you vs:

* workflow tools,
* bookkeeping automation,
* and prep/time-saving tooling.

Investors will want evidence that the price maps to **hours saved or revenue created** per client. Given current “back-to-fundamentals,” you need at least 3–5 design partner quotes or LOIs indicating willingness to pay at/near that level. ([ICONIQ Capital][1])

**4) The scariest risk is “onboarding capacity is the throttle”**
You correctly identified the constraint—but investors will ask:

* What reduces onboarding time from 45 → 21 days?
* What % of onboarding becomes self-serve via templates/runbooks?
* What is the target “implementation hours per firm” at scale?

Right now, the plan states the problem but doesn’t show the scaling mechanism with numbers.

## 3) The 5 questions every angel/seed will ask you immediately

1. **Why you win vs incumbents + AI-native entrants?**
   Your answer should be: *“Proven completeness + TB control surface + explicit uncertainty boundary + firm-led standardization.”* But you need a crisp competitive frame.

2. **Is the product a software business or a services business in disguise?**
   You must show that the 85% boundary + workflow/tasks + templates reduce human involvement over time.

3. **Can you actually distribute through firms at scale?**
   Show early funnel + conversion + expansion mechanics.

4. **What does retention look like?**
   In a firm-led model, retention is: “does the firm keep expanding clients?” Your key metric (“clients per firm over time”) is correct—make it the headline KPI.

5. **What’s the first repeatable use case?**
   Tax-prep-driven preparation is the wedge; don’t dilute it with forecasting/modeling until you have repeatability.
