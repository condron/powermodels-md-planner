# The Accounting Podcast - Why AI Bookkeeping Still Doesn't Work

## Source
**Podcast**: The Accounting Podcast  
**Episode**: SuiteWorld Recap and Bookkeeping Startup Raises $200M  
**Date**: September 17, 2024  
**Industry context**: Discussion of NetSuite's SuiteWorld conference and $200M raise by AI bookkeeping startup

---

## Executive Summary

This episode **dismantles the myth that AI and capital can quickly replace bookkeeping and accounting**. Key thesis:

> "Expense categorization is easy. Financial truth is not. Accruals, judgment, trust, and accountability remain the core — and they are stubbornly human."

**Critical validation for PowerModels**:
- 85/15 boundary is exactly right
- Professional judgment preservation is strategic necessity
- Event-sourced "financial truth" addresses core industry problem
- Services vs software economics are structural (must avoid services creep)

## Key Industry Insights

### 1. ERP and Accounting Software is Extraordinarily Hard

**Evidence**:
- **NetSuite**: 25+ years old, still has GL and workflow gaps
- **Mature platforms** (SAP, Oracle): Still incomplete after decades
- **Startup narrative**: "Can rebuild accounting systems quickly with modern tools" is FALSE

**Quote**:
> "NetSuite's scale and longevity are not signs of stagnation; they are evidence of complexity."

**Implication for PowerModels**:
- Event-sourced architecture is NOT overkill — it addresses real complexity
- Building "ledger-grade" systems justifies technical investment
- Claiming "easy rebuild" would damage credibility with practitioners

---

### 2. Bookkeeping Startups Oversell Automation

**What most "AI bookkeeping" actually does**:
- Expense categorization (transaction ingestion + category guessing)
- Post to GL
- **That's it** — this is "a narrow slice of the problem"

**What they avoid (the hard work)**:
- Accruals and deferrals
- Revenue recognition
- Period accuracy
- Judgment-based adjustments

**Quote**:
> "Most so-called 'automated bookkeeping' platforms are performing expense categorization, not accounting."

**Implication for PowerModels**:
- **Competitive positioning**: We do tax preparation (not just categorization)
- **85% boundary**: Explicitly acknowledges what automation CAN'T do
- **Differentiation**: We don't oversell — we preserve professional judgment

---

### 3. Accrual Accounting Remains UNSOLVED

**No startup has demonstrated reliable automation for**:
- Accruals and deferrals
- Revenue recognition  
- Period accuracy
- Judgment-based adjustments

**Quote**:
> "AI has not solved this. Claims that it has are marketing, not evidence."

**Implication for PowerModels**:
- Tax prep wedge is SAFER than full accrual automation
- 85/15 boundary protects from overpromising
- Event sourcing enables "rewind/refresh" when judgment changes
- We don't claim to automate professional judgment

### 5. General Ledgers are Extraordinarily Difficult

**Building functional GL requires**:
- Technical complexity
- Exception-driven logic
- Regulatory and jurisdiction-dependent rules

**Quote**:
> "Even mature systems like NetSuite, SAP, and Oracle are incomplete. The idea that a startup can 'quickly build a better GL' ignores decades of evidence."

**Implication for PowerModels**:
- Event-sourced architecture is appropriate complexity
- NOT trying to "replace QB/Xero GL" — import and enhance
- Platform-independent after import (event stream = source of truth)
- Respects that GL complexity is real

---

### 6. Bookkeeping is People-Heavy Business (Structural Economics)

**Core dilemma**:
- **Remove humans** → accuracy fails
- **Keep humans** → margins collapse

**Quote**:
> "This is why accounting firms grow steadily but rarely achieve SaaS-level multiples. The economics are structural, not managerial."

**Implication for PowerModels**:
- **Critical strategic insight**: 85/15 boundary is economics-driven, not just positioning
- **Firm-first GTM**: Leverage firm's human judgment (don't replace it)
- **Software margins**: AI to 85% enables software economics, 15% stays with firm
- **Investor concern**: "Is this software or services?" → 85/15 is the answer

---

### 7. AI is Powerful Assistant — NOT Replacement

**AI is valuable for**:
- Research
- Drafting
- Pattern recognition
- Workflow assistance

**AI is NOT reliable for**:
- Judgment
- Accountability
- Edge cases
- **Financial truth**

**Quote**:
> "Positioning AI as a replacement for accountants misunderstands both AI and accounting."

**Implication for PowerModels**:
- **"AI to empower accountants, not replace them"** = EXACTLY RIGHT
- Industry is converging on this position
- Competitive advantage: We got positioning correct from start
- Investor validation: Market understands this now (Sept 2024)

---

### 8. NetSuite Dominance is Trust-Based

**Why NetSuite persists despite complaints**:
- No meaningful user exodus
- Investors and boards view as "safe choice"
- Switching risk > dissatisfaction

**Quote**:
> "This highlights how entrenched accounting platforms really are."

**Implication for PowerModels**:
- **Not trying to replace** QB/Xero/NetSuite
- **Import and enhance** strategy respects platform lock-in
- **Event-sourced independence** offers flexibility without rip-and-replace risk
- **Trust**: Build on existing platforms, don't fight them

---

### 9. Profession Persists Because Work Reflects Reality

**Why accounting is hard to automate**:
- Incomplete data
- Human behavior
- Timing mismatches
- Regulatory constraints

**Quote**:
> "If accounting were easy to automate end-to-end, it would have been done already."

**Implication for PowerModels**:
- **85/15 boundary acknowledges reality**
- Professional judgment exists because businesses are messy
- AI helps with patterns, professionals handle exceptions
- This is why event sourcing matters (captures reality, enables replay)
