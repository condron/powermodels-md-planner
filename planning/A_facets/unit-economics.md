# Unit Economics & Capital Efficiency

## Description

PowerModels' firm-first GTM model creates fundamentally different unit economics than traditional SMB SaaS. Instead of linear CAC per customer, acquisition cost is amortized across entire firm client portfolios, creating a highly leveraged growth model.

## Core Economic Model

### Revenue Structure — Three-Tier Pricing

| Tier | Client Type | Price/mo | QB Equivalent | Current Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Micro/1099** | 1099 contractors, minimal books | $20/mo | QB Simple Start | ✅ Product focus now, Bancroft validated |
| **Solo/Partnership** | Solo proprietors, partnerships | $50-100/mo | QB Essentials/Plus | 🟡 Next phase |
| **SMB** | Small businesses, full books | $200/mo | QB Advanced | 🟡 Future |

- **Firm-controlled**: Firm passes cost through to clients (same as QB billing model)
- **Pricing anchored to QB**: Clients already accept these price points
- **Monthly billing**: Predictable recurring revenue
- **Growth path**: Start with micro tier, expand to solo/partnership and SMB as product matures

### Cost Structure
- **CAC**: Incurred once per firm (not per client)
- **Revenue expansion**: As firm adds clients to platform
- **Retention driver**: Workflow integration and record reuse (not contractual lock-in)
- **Sales efficiency**: One firm sale → many client deployments

## The Leverage Mechanism

```
Traditional SMB SaaS:          PowerModels Firm-First:
CAC × N customers              CAC × 1 firm
Revenue × N                    Revenue × (clients per firm)
Linear scaling                 Leveraged scaling
```

### Example Economics (Blended Portfolio)

Typical small firm portfolio (Bancroft model):
```
150 micro/1099 clients  × $20/mo  = $3,000/mo
 30 solo/partnership     × $75/mo  = $2,250/mo
 10 SMB clients          × $200/mo = $2,000/mo
─────────────────────────────────────────────
190 clients total                   $7,250/mo MRR
                                   $87,000/yr ARR per firm
```

Even micro-only (current product focus):
```
200 micro/1099 clients  × $20/mo  = $4,000/mo MRR
                                   $48,000/yr ARR per firm
```

Compare to SMB SaaS:
- 200 individual sales @ $2K CAC each = $400K total CAC
- Same $4K/month revenue
- Firm-first: single CAC, same revenue

## Path to Breakeven

**Base operating plan reaches cash-flow breakeven through:**
1. Firm adoption (not new product lines)
2. Client rollout within firms (not pricing expansion)
3. Standardization and reuse (not high-velocity selling)

**No reliance on:**
- New modules or upsells
- Price increases
- Venture-scale marketing spend
- SMB self-serve funnels

## Capital Efficiency Drivers

### Low Customer Acquisition Cost (per revenue dollar)
- Sell once to firm → expand across portfolio
- Trust-based adoption → lower sales friction
- Professional networks → referral-driven growth

### High Customer Lifetime Value
- Workflow integration → sticky retention
- Multi-year standardization → durable relationships
- Expansion within firm → growing ARPU

### Predictable Growth
- Growth constrained by onboarding capacity, not marketing spend
- Disciplined scaling (not growth-at-all-costs)
- Organic expansion (firm-controlled, not sales-pushed)

## Client Dashboard & Monthly Cadence

### Client Dashboard Website
- End-clients see critical information from the books the accountant manages each month
- **Firm value**: Transparency builds client trust, reduces "where are my books?" inquiries
- **Client value**: Visibility into financial position without bothering the accountant
- **Strategic value**: The dashboard becomes the **sell-through route for the forecasting product** — full business cash and revenue forecasting with drift metrics. Clients who see their books monthly will naturally want to see where they're headed.

### Monthly Update Cadence
- **Critical for firms**: Monthly book updates prevent work explosions at critical times (tax season, audits, quarterly filings)
- Without monthly updates: 12 months of accumulated work hits at once = the "100-hour problem" (Stewart data point)
- With monthly updates: work is spread evenly, each month is incremental, no surprises
- **Retention mechanism**: Monthly cadence creates habitual usage and ongoing value delivery
- **Revenue justification**: $20-200/mo is cheap insurance against a multi-thousand-dollar work explosion

### Product Expansion Sequence
```
Phase 1 (NOW):    Micro/1099 → monthly books + client dashboard
Phase 2:          Solo/Partnership → richer books + enhanced dashboard
Phase 3:          SMB → full books + forecasting via dashboard
Phase 4:          Forecasting product sold through client dashboard
                  (cash forecasting, revenue forecasting, drift metrics)
```

The client dashboard is the trojan horse for the forecasting product. Clients who see monthly books will want projections. The dashboard is already there.

## Net Revenue Retention (NRR) — Three Expansion Vectors

The product structure creates **NRR > 100%** (increasing revenue retention) through three independent expansion vectors, none of which require new customer acquisition:

| Vector | Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. More clients per firm** | Firm adds clients to platform, zero new CAC | Bancroft: several hundred clients, onboards more over time |
| **2. Client tier upgrades** | Micro ($20) → Solo ($50-100) → SMB ($200) as businesses grow | 1099 contractor becomes LLC, moves to Solo tier |
| **3. Forecasting upsell via dashboard** | Monthly books → client wants projections → paid upgrade | Client sees monthly dashboard, adds cash/revenue forecasting with drift metrics |

**Why this matters for investors:**
- NRR > 100% = revenue grows from existing base without new logos
- "Negative churn" — even if some clients leave, revenue still grows
- Each vector is independent — they compound
- Vector 3 (forecasting) is the strongest because it's a new product sold through an existing surface with zero sales motion
- This is the metric that separates "good SaaS" from "great SaaS" at Series A

**Illustrative NRR model (single firm, Year 1 → Year 2):**
```
Year 1: 150 micro × $20 = $3,000/mo
Year 2: 170 micro × $20 = $3,400/mo  (more clients)
      +  15 upgraded to solo × $75    = $1,125/mo  (tier upgrades)
      +  50 forecasting add-ons × $10 = $500/mo    (dashboard upsell)
                                       ─────────
Year 2 MRR from same firm:             $5,025/mo
NRR: 168% ($5,025 / $3,000)
```

## Strategic Implications

### Growth Velocity
- **Speed measured by**: Time to completed cycle + rollout depth (not logo count)
- **Expansion is mechanical**: Once firm standardizes, growth becomes operational not promotional
- **Quality over quantity**: 10 deep firm relationships > 100 shallow SMB customers

### Capital Requirements
- **Lower burn**: No high-velocity SMB acquisition engine needed
- **Longer runway**: Disciplined growth extends cash runway
- **Milestone-driven**: Capital accelerates proven motion, doesn't fund speculation

### Investor Appeal
- **Capital efficient**: Leveraged model reduces dilution
- **Durable growth**: Firm relationships are sticky and compound
- **Clear path to profitability**: Not dependent on constant fundraising

## Risks

- **Slower initial growth**: Longer sales cycles, deeper relationships
- **Firm concentration**: Loss of one firm = loss of many clients
- **Onboarding capacity**: Growth constrained by ability to deliver quality implementations
