Why “AI Bookkeeping” Still Doesn’t Work — and What SuiteWorld Reveals About the Reality of Accounting Software

Podcast: The Accounting Podcast
Episode: SuiteWorld Recap and Book-keeping Startup Raises $200M
Date: September 17, 2024

⸻

Executive Summary — Key Points from the Full Episode

SuiteWorld reinforces how hard ERP and accounting software really is

NetSuite’s scale and longevity are not signs of stagnation; they are evidence of complexity. After more than 25 years, the platform still has gaps in its GL and workflows. That reality undercuts the common startup narrative that accounting systems can be “rebuilt quickly” with modern tools or AI.

⸻

Bookkeeping startups are overselling what automation actually does

Most so-called “automated bookkeeping” platforms are performing expense categorization, not accounting. They ingest transactions, guess categories, and post to a GL. That is a narrow slice of the problem and avoids the hardest work.

⸻

Accrual accounting remains the unsolved problem

No startup has demonstrated reliable, scalable automation for:
	•	Accruals and deferrals
	•	Revenue recognition
	•	Period accuracy
	•	Judgment-based adjustments

AI has not solved this. Claims that it has are marketing, not evidence.

⸻

Massive fundraising does not equal technical validation

The $200M raise discussed reflects investor optimism, not proof of feasibility. Investors are betting that founders will eventually figure out:
	•	Full GL logic
	•	Exception handling
	•	Compliance nuance
	•	Human judgment replacement

Those with real firm experience are far more skeptical — for good reason.

⸻

General ledgers are extraordinarily difficult to build

Building a functional GL is:
	•	Technically complex
	•	Exception-driven
	•	Regulatory and jurisdiction-dependent

Even mature systems like NetSuite, SAP, and Oracle are incomplete. The idea that a startup can “quickly build a better GL” ignores decades of evidence.

⸻

Bookkeeping is still a people-heavy business

True bookkeeping and accounting do not scale like software:
	•	Remove humans → accuracy fails
	•	Keep humans → margins collapse

This is why accounting firms grow steadily but rarely achieve SaaS-level multiples. The economics are structural, not managerial.

⸻

AI is a powerful assistant — not a replacement

AI is valuable for:
	•	Research
	•	Drafting
	•	Pattern recognition
	•	Workflow assistance

It is not reliable for:
	•	Judgment
	•	Accountability
	•	Edge cases
	•	Financial truth

Positioning AI as a replacement for accountants misunderstands both AI and accounting.

⸻

NetSuite’s dominance is based on trust, not love

Users may complain about cost and complexity, but:
	•	There is no meaningful user exodus
	•	Investors and boards view NetSuite as the “safe” choice
	•	Switching risk outweighs dissatisfaction

This highlights how entrenched accounting platforms really are.

⸻

The profession persists because the work reflects reality

Accounting is difficult because businesses are messy:
	•	Incomplete data
	•	Human behavior
	•	Timing mismatches
	•	Regulatory constraints

If accounting were easy to automate end-to-end, it would have been done already.

⸻

Bottom Line

The episode dismantles the myth that AI and capital can quickly replace bookkeeping and accounting. Expense categorization is easy. Financial truth is not. Accruals, judgment, trust, and accountability remain the core — and they are stubbornly human.
