While the Pennsylvania Society of Tax & Accounting Professionals (PSTAP) does not release a "live" ledger of the exact number of client firms its members represent, we can calculate a high-confidence estimate based on their membership data and industry testimony.
PSTAP represents approximately 2,000 practitioners who are almost exclusively in public practice. Based on their impact statements and industry averages, PSTAP members collectively represent:
1. Estimated Total Client Firm Reach
Total Business/Employer Clients: ~200,000 to 250,000 small businesses.
Total Individual Taxpayers: Tens of thousands (PSTAP's official legislative testimony states they provide services to "tens of thousands of individual taxpayers, small business owners, and employers").
2. Breakdown by Member Practice
Because PSTAP focuses on the "small firm" niche, we can look at the average client-to-practitioner ratio for this specific demographic:
• Business Clients: Industry data suggests there is a pool of about 125 business clients for every small accounting firm.1 Multiplied by PSTAP's ~2,000 members, this aligns with a reach of roughly 250,000 client firms.


• High-Touch Advisory: A typical PSTAP member focusing on business advisory usually manages a core group of 50 "highest level" business clients per partner/owner.
• Tax Focus: Most PSTAP members prioritize tax services, where "client firms" include S-Corps, LLCs, and small partnerships. A single solo practitioner in the society often handles between 100 and 200 business entities in addition to individual 1040 returns.
3. Economic Impact of PSTAP Member Firms
PSTAP members are a "unifying force" for small firms in Pennsylvania.2 The businesses they represent are primarily:

Small Employers: Local PA businesses with 1–20 employees.
Main Street Entities: Restaurants, retail, professional services, and contractors throughout Pennsylvania's 9 regional chapters.
Self-Employed Professionals: Independent contractors and solo entrepreneurs.
