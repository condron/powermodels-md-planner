# Seed / Series A Investors

## Description

Seed and Series A investors provide growth capital for companies with early traction and repeatable go-to-market motion. They focus on product-market fit, unit economics, competitive positioning, and path to scale. They expect disciplined execution toward Series B milestones.

## Primary Concerns

- **Product-market fit**: Is there clear evidence customers want this and will pay for it?
- **Go-to-market repeatability**: Can the company acquire customers predictably?
- **Unit economics**: What's the CAC, LTV, payback period? Do economics improve with scale?
- **Competitive moats**: What's defensible? Why won't incumbents or well-funded startups win?
- **Team strength**: Can this team scale from 5 to 50 people while maintaining quality?
- **Capital efficiency**: How much capital needed to reach next inflection point?
- **Market size**: Is TAM large enough for venture-scale outcomes ($100M+ ARR potential)?

## Communication Preferences

- **Language**: Growth metrics and business model (ARR, NRR, CAC/LTV, gross margin, burn multiple)
- **Proof points**: Customer cohorts, sales funnel data, expansion metrics, competitive differentiation
- **Format**: Investor decks, monthly metrics updates, board observer participation
- **Cadence**: Monthly metrics emails, quarterly deep-dives, annual strategic planning

## Decision Factors

- **Traction proof**: Need 3-5+ reference customers, ideally with expansion
- **Economic viability**: Unit economics must pencil to profitability at scale
- **Founder-market fit**: Team must demonstrate deep domain expertise
- **Competitive differentiation**: Must articulate why this wins vs alternatives
- **Capital plan credibility**: Milestones must be achievable with requested capital
- **Series B line-of-sight**: Path to $5-10M ARR and institutional round must be clear
- **Firm-first model validation**: Must prove one firm can expand across multiple clients
