# Tax Preparers & CPAs

## Description

Tax preparers and CPAs are the hands-on professionals who perform preparation, review, and sign-off work. They prioritize correctness, defensibility, and workflow efficiency, and are highly sensitive to tools that obscure judgment or introduce new risks.

## Primary Concerns

- **Correctness and trust**: Will this tool make mistakes I'll be liable for?
- **Transparency**: Can I see what the system did and why?
- **Professional control**: Does this preserve my judgment or try to replace it?
- **Workload**: Will this actually save time or create new work?
- **Review efficiency**: Are working papers clear and defensible?
- **Client confidence**: Can I explain adjustments and positions to clients?
- **Adoption friction**: How long to learn? Will it slow me down initially?

## Communication Preferences

- **Language**: Accounting terminology, not software jargon (schedules, working papers, reconciliation, audit trail)
- **Proof points**: Working paper samples, side-by-side workflow comparisons, testimonials from peer CPAs
- **Format**: Hands-on demos, walkthrough of actual client scenarios
- **Cadence**: Pre-season training, in-season support, post-season retrospectives

## Decision Factors

- **Explicit uncertainty**: System must flag transactions it cannot confidently classify
- **No black boxes**: Every categorization and adjustment must be traceable
- **Workflow fit**: Must integrate into existing review process, not replace it
- **85% boundary**: Judgment, interpretation, and sign-off must remain with the professional
- **Peer validation**: Trust comes from seeing other CPAs successfully use the platform
- **Seasonal pressure**: Cannot disrupt workflow during peak season; pilots must run in off-season or with low-risk clients
